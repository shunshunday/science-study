- A concrete mod-asset example: Station Parts Expansion Redux explicitly reserves rights to its `.dds`, `.png` and `.mu` art assets, despite licensing other files separately: https://github.com/post-kerbin-mining-corporation/StationPartsExpansionRedux

## Goal
Replace the current placeholder/custom part catalog in `game.js` with a complete Kerbal Space Program 1 parts catalog (base game plus Making History and Breaking Ground), while using only 3D assets whose redistribution and web-hosting permissions are explicit.

## Licensing findings
- KSP's official website links to the official wiki and community modding resources: https://kerbalspaceprogram.com/
- KSP stock game assets are not automatically licensed for redistribution just because a person owns the game. Do not extract or publish stock meshes/textures from the game without written permission or a license that expressly allows redistribution.
- Mod assets must be checked individually. A mod's source code license does not necessarily cover its art assets. For example, Station Parts Expansion Redux explicitly says its .dds, .png and .mu art assets are All Rights Reserved: https://github.com/post-kerbin-mining-corporation/StationPartsExpansionRedux
- Some mod packs use different licenses and permissions. Do not assume that one mod's license applies to another mod or to the stock game.
- The KSP Community repositories can help discover mod parts, but discovery/listing is not itself permission to reuse models: https://github.com/kspcommunity

## Import acceptance checklist
A model can be added only after recording:
1. Exact source URL and creator.
2. Exact license applying to the model and textures (not just the code).
3. Whether redistribution, modification, conversion to glTF/GLB, and hosting on GitHub Pages are permitted.
4. Required attribution and license text.
5. Any non-commercial, share-alike, or other restrictions.
6. A local asset filename and a mapping to the corresponding KSP part name/variant.

## Planned catalog scope
- Base game: command pods, probe cores, fuel tanks, engines, boosters, decouplers, adapters, structural parts, aerodynamic parts, landing gear, wheels, parachutes, batteries, solar panels, antennas, science equipment, thermal parts, docking parts, cargo bays, utility parts, and resource/mining parts.
- Making History: DLC-exclusive parts, kept identifiable as DLC.
- Breaking Ground: robotics parts and deployable science/surface equipment, kept identifiable as DLC.
- Legacy/obsolete parts should be listed separately rather than silently mixed into the current stock catalog.

## Safe implementation sequence
1. Build a verified stock + DLC part-name/stat catalog and mark each item by source (base game / Making History / Breaking Ground / legacy).
2. Find or commission redistributable models for each item; record provenance and license before importing.
3. Implement model-to-part mapping and preserve attachment nodes, dimensions, mass, fuel, thrust, and special behavior.
4. Remove placeholder catalog entries only after the replacement catalog and models are complete and checked.

- Important accuracy boundary: the new meshes are original KSP-inspired approximations, not official KSP models. They are usable for visual assembly, but must not be represented as exact KSP 1.12.5 performance data.
